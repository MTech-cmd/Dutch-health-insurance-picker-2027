## How to interpret this table

- Lowest premium does not automatically mean best value.
- A percentage reimbursement is not enough: check the calculation base, caps, exclusions, prior authorisation and whether a provider is contracted.
- A stated care pathway does not guarantee an appointment or short waiting time.
- Provider network percentages are fictional company-reported metrics, not independently audited.
- Complaint rates are company-reported and may use different definitions in real-world comparisons.
- Supplementary insurance benefits may have eligibility rules, waiting periods, annual limits and exclusions.
- The basic policy and supplementary policy must be assessed separately.

## Scenario prompts

1. If you need a specific gender-affirming provider, which contract and referral details would you verify?
2. If you use frequent physiotherapy and dental care, compare the extra premium with actual expected reimbursements.
3. If you live outside Kompas's core provinces, does its regional advantage still matter?
4. If you choose Rivierzorg for the low premium, could one non-contracted treatment create a large bill?
5. Which statements are supported by official policy documents, and which are only company marketing claims?

## No overall winner is assigned

The best choice depends on location, current providers, expected care, policy wording, affordability and tolerance for non-contracted-care risk. This simulation intentionally leaves the final choice to the reader.
